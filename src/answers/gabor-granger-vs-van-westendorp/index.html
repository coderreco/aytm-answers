Gabor-Granger vs. Van Westendorp: which pricing method to use?

Last updated: 2025-11-14

TL;DR: Gabor-Granger measures price elasticity by testing willingness to buy at specific price points. Van Westendorp captures price perceptions and acceptable price ranges using four simple questions. Use Gabor-Granger when you need demand curves or revenue optimization; use Van Westendorp when you need perception-based ranges or early-stage pricing direction.

What is Gabor-Granger?

Gabor-Granger is a pricing technique where respondents are shown a product or concept and asked if they would buy it at a specific price. Depending on their answer, they are shown higher or lower prices.

What Gabor-Granger tells you

  • Price elasticity
  • Purchase likelihood at each tested price
  • Demand curve
  • Revenue curve & optimal price point

Best use cases

  • Products with clear value and known features
  • Situations where you must select a specific price
  • Testing multiple price points or tiers

What is Van Westendorp?

Van Westendorp’s Price Sensitivity Meter (PSM) asks four open-ended questions to find the acceptable price range for a product.

The four Van Westendorp questions

  • Too cheap
  • Cheap / good value
  • Expensive
  • Too expensive

What Van Westendorp tells you

  • Acceptable price range (between “too cheap” and “too expensive” intersections)
  • Perceived value
  • Price thresholds and psychological barriers

Best use cases

  • Early-stage pricing for new products
  • Understanding price fairness perceptions
  • Situations where respondents do not yet know true value

Key differences

MethodWhat it measuresWhen it's best
Gabor-GrangerDemand + elasticityOptimizing price for revenue
Van WestendorpPerception + acceptable rangesExploratory pricing or early stages

Which one should you choose?

  • Use Gabor-Granger when you want hard behavioral measures (“would you buy at $X?”).
  • Use Van Westendorp when you want sentiment-based pricing guidance.
  • Use both if you need a triangulated pricing strategy.

FAQs

Can you run both in the same study?

Yes—many teams do this to get both a perception range and a demand curve.

Do these methods predict real-world sales?

They predict intent, not guaranteed sales, but are directionally useful for pricing decisions.
