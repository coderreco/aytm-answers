Market landscape surveys: how to map categories and competitors

Last updated: 2025-11-14

TL;DR: Market landscape surveys help you understand the size, shape, and dynamics of a category—who competes, how customers behave, what needs are unmet, and where whitespace opportunities exist. Use them to set strategy, refine positioning, and identify growth pathways.

What is a market landscape survey?

A market landscape study provides a high-level overview of a category, covering:

  • Category size and participation
  • Competitors considered and used
  • Customer needs and pain points
  • Perceived strengths and weaknesses of brands
  • Usage occasions and decision drivers
  • Segmentation and audience clustering

Common goals of a landscape survey

  • Identify whitespace and opportunities
  • Understand competitors’ positioning
  • Support go-to-market or product strategy
  • Size demand for segments or occasions
  • Map unmet needs
  • Guide innovation pipelines

What to include in a market landscape study

1. Category overview

  • Awareness and familiarity
  • General sentiment toward the category
  • Adoption barriers

2. Brand funnel and competitive set

  • Brands considered, used, preferred
  • Brand strengths and weaknesses
  • Brand perception drivers

3. Customer needs & pain points

  • Functional and emotional needs
  • What’s missing in existing solutions
  • Desired improvements

4. Usage occasions

Understanding when and how customers engage with the category:

  • Frequency
  • Contexts
  • Triggers
  • Alternatives used

5. Segmentation

Identify natural clusters of users based on attitudes, behaviors, or needs.

6. Choice drivers

  • Top benefits sought
  • Barriers to choosing your brand
  • Reasons for switching

Analyzing your landscape

  • Spot whitespace opportunities
  • Identify high-value segments
  • Benchmark brand performance
  • Compare usage occasions and triggers
  • Inform product and positioning roadmaps

FAQs

How big should a landscape survey be?

Most are 800–2,000 respondents depending on complexity and segment depth.

How often should companies run these?

Annually or biannually, unless a category is changing rapidly.

Are landscape studies the same as U&A?

They’re related—U&A studies are deeper and more detailed, while landscape surveys are higher-level and more strategic.
