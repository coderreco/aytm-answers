Customer satisfaction surveys: a practical guide

Last updated: 2025-11-14

TL;DR: Customer satisfaction surveys help you understand whether your product, service, or experience is meeting expectations. Keep them short, ask direct questions about satisfaction, effort, and likelihood to recommend, and pair ratings with open-ends to uncover drivers and improvements.

What are customer satisfaction surveys?

Customer satisfaction (CSAT) surveys measure how satisfied customers are with a product, service, or experience. They’re often used for:

  • Understanding customer needs and pain points
  • Evaluating service interactions or product updates
  • Identifying loyalty drivers and churn risks
  • Tracking satisfaction trends over time

When to use customer satisfaction surveys

CSAT surveys are usually fielded at key “moments of truth,” including:

  • After a purchase or transaction
  • After customer support interactions
  • After product onboarding
  • Periodically for ongoing relationship feedback

Core CSAT metrics to include

Most surveys include the following measures:

1. Overall satisfaction (CSAT)

“Overall, how satisfied are you with [brand/product/service]?”

2. Likelihood to recommend (NPS)

Net Promoter Score measures the likelihood that a customer would recommend your brand.

3. Customer effort score (CES)

“How easy was it to accomplish your goal today?” CES helps identify friction areas.

4. Satisfaction with key drivers

  • Quality
  • Ease of use
  • Value for money
  • Support experience
  • Speed or reliability

5. Why responses (open-ends)

Open-ended questions help explain why customers rated you positively or negatively.

How to design a strong CSAT survey

  1. Define what you want to learn
    Are you improving onboarding, support, or product experience? Keep your survey focused.
  2. Keep it short
    5–10 questions is enough for most CSAT surveys.
  3. Use simple scales
    Consistent rating scales make tracking clearer.
  4. Ask at the right time
    Send surveys immediately after key interactions to get accurate responses.
  5. Include relevant demographic or behavioral questions
    These help diagnose differences across segments.

Analyzing CSAT results

Focus on:

  • Topline scores (CSAT, NPS, CES)
  • Segment differences (which groups are happiest or struggling)
  • Key driver analysis (what influences satisfaction)
  • Open-ended themes

Common pitfalls

  • Surveys that are too long or too complicated
  • Sending surveys too long after the experience
  • Not following up on negative or low-effort scores
  • Overreacting to small sample sizes

FAQs

How often should we run CSAT surveys?

After key events (support interactions, purchases) and periodically (quarterly or semi-annually) for relationship health.

How big should the sample be?

Even small sample sizes can be useful if they reflect the right moments, but aim for at least 100–200 responses per major channel.

Should CSAT be anonymous?

It depends on your feedback loop. Anonymous surveys can increase honesty, but attributed surveys help close the loop with customers.
