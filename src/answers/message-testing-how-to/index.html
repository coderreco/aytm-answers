Message testing: how to evaluate and refine your story

Last updated: 2025-11-14

TL;DR: Message testing helps you understand which messages resonate, which fall flat, and how to tailor your story to different audiences. Start with a clear set of hypotheses, standardize the structure of your messages, test with the right audience, and use both ratings and open-ends to refine your narrative.

What is message testing?

Message testing is research focused on the words you use to describe your product, service, or idea. You might test:

  • Value propositions and positioning statements
  • Benefit statements and proof points
  • Taglines and headlines
  • Differentiation or “why us” statements

The goal is to identify which messages are most compelling, believable, and differentiating for specific audiences.

When to run message testing

Message testing is helpful when you are:

  • Developing a new brand or product positioning
  • Refining website, sales, or campaign copy
  • Translating complex features into benefits
  • Localizing messages for new segments or markets

How to structure messages for testing

Messages are easier to compare when they follow a consistent pattern. A simple structure is:

  • Headline: the core idea or promise
  • Support sentence: how you deliver that benefit
  • Proof point (optional): data, social proof, or credibility

Use similar length and tone across messages so performance differences reflect content, not format.

Design choices for message testing

On a platform like AYTM, common designs include:

  • Rating-only: Each respondent rates all messages on scales such as clarity, relevance, and impact.
  • Rating + ranking: Respondents rate messages, then rank their top choices.
  • MaxDiff: Respondents select most/least appealing messages from sets to derive a priority order.
  • Monadic blocks: Subsets of messages per respondent to manage survey length.

Key metrics to capture

Typical question areas include:

  • Appeal: “How appealing is this message?”
  • Relevance: “How relevant is this to you / your organization?”
  • Clarity: “How easy is this to understand?”
  • Differentiation: “How different does this feel from other options?”
  • Believability: “How believable is this claim?”
  • Action intent: “How likely would this make you consider / learn more / talk to sales?”
  • Open-ended feedback: “What, if anything, stands out to you about this message?”

Analyzing message testing results

Look for patterns across:

  • Top performers: messages that consistently score high on appeal, relevance, and clarity.
  • Segments: how different audiences respond (e.g., current vs. prospective customers).
  • Drivers: which qualities (e.g., clarity vs. uniqueness) correlate most with action intent.
  • Verbatims: themes in “like most / like least” feedback.

Use this to create a short, focused message hierarchy—your core story plus supporting proof points.

Common pitfalls

  • Testing too many messages in one survey and causing fatigue
  • Comparing messages with very different lengths or levels of detail
  • Ignoring segment-specific favorites
  • Over-indexing on clever wording vs. clear benefit

FAQs

How many messages should we test at once?

10–20 is common, especially with designs that rotate subsets or use MaxDiff. For very large lists, consider pre-filtering internally.

Can we test messages with and without branding?

Yes. You can test unbranded for pure idea strength, then branded to see how your name affects perceptions.

How do we turn results into final copy?

Use the winning message elements—the core ideas and phrases—as building blocks. Combine them into a structured narrative for web, sales, and campaigns rather than using survey stimuli verbatim.
